A Historical Review of AI-Based Music Composition: From Local Pattern Prediction to Holistic Audio Generation

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
인공지능연구 논문지 7(3), 37–66

AI-based music composition has evolved from explicit rule encoding and local symbolic prediction to end-to-end generation of integrated musical audio. This historical review examines representative systems that either introduced a distinct generative paradigm or achieved practical deployment. It traces the progression from rule-based and stochastic models through recurrent networks and Transformers to waveform, latent-audio, diffusion, flow-matching, hybrid, and multimodal approaches. Early models reproduced local transitions among notes, chords, and rhythms but struggled with long-range form and thematic development. Recurrent and attention-based architectures expanded temporal context, yet largely remained within symbolic prediction. Audio-level models shifted the generative target from musical instructions to sound itself, enabling joint modeling of structure, performance, timbre, texture, and acoustic realization. The review argues that the key historical transition was not simply an increase in model capacity, but a change in representational scope: from decomposing music into human-defined units to learning high-dimensional relationships more holistically. Current systems still face unresolved problems in long-term planning, fine-grained control, editability, and human–AI collaboration. Nevertheless, their development reflects a broader movement from automating compositional procedures toward modeling increasingly integrated stages of musical ideation and sound formation.

Byunghyun Ban (2026)

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
ResearchGate · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

Post-publication integrity concerns are often treated as problems of textual similarity, missing citation, or minor correctable error. However, some cases involve deeper forms of scholarly overlap that cannot be remedied through limited post-publication modification. This paper presents a case study of a post-publication complaint involving a 2022 preprint and a subsequent 2023 journal article that was later retracted. The official retraction notice states that an editorial investigation confirmed a high level of overlap between the subsequent article and the prior publication, including similarities in methodology, figures, layout, paper structure, and overall study topic, without appropriate acknowledgement and citation. The central argument is that post-publication modification may be insufficient when the integrity concern extends beyond wording to methodology, visual materials, manuscript structure, attribution, and data provenance. In such cases, correction or citation addition cannot substitute for a full editorial investigation and, where appropriate, retraction.

The phenotype control kernel of a biomolecular regulatory network

Sang-Mok Choo, Byunghyun Ban, Jae Il Joo, Kwang-Hyun Cho* (2018)
BMC Systems Biology  (SCI)

Background: Controlling complex molecular regulatory networks is getting a growing attention as it can provide a systematic way of driving any cellular state to a desired cell phenotypic state. A number of recent studies suggested various control methods, but there is still deficiency in finding out practically useful control targets that ensure convergence of any initial network state to one of attractor states corresponding to a desired cell phenotype. Results: To find out practically useful control targets, we introduce a new concept of phenotype control kernel (PCK) for a Boolean network, defined as the collection of all minimal sets of control nodes having their fixed state values that can generate all possible control sets which eventually drive any initial state to one of attractor states corresponding to a particular cell phenotype of interest. We also present a detailed method with which we can identify PCK in a systematic way based on the layered network and converging tree of a given network. We identify all candidates for control nodes from the layered network and then hierarchically search for all possible minimal sets by using the converging tree. We show the usefulness of PCK by applying it to cell proliferation and apoptosis signaling networks and comparing the results with other control methods. PCK is the unique control method for Boolean network models that can be used to identify all possible minimal sets of control nodes. Interestingly, many of the minimal sets have only one or two control nodes. Conclusions: Based on the new concept of PCK, we can identify all possible minimal sets of control nodes that can drive any molecular network state to one of multiple attractor states representing a same desired cell phenotype.

대립 신경망을 이용한 심혈관 단층촬영 영상 표지 방법

반병현(2018)
한국과학기술원 (석사학위논문)

Bio-medical image segmentation, which is an emerging technique for an assistive way for quick and deliberate diagnosis, is to separate a biological or medical image into different semantic parts. Contemporary machine learning based segmentation techniques depend on statistical approach to minimize the objective functions which ensure statistically to distinguish each pixel into one of the desired classes. However, thus philosophy consistently fails when the numbers of pixels for those class meet severe imbalance and presents prediction errors on all around the given input. This thesis provides a robust solution for Bio-medical image segmentation with adversarial networks, which are composed of a segmentor based on fully convolutional neural networks, image windowing technique and a discriminator which interferes the segmentor’s learning, to acquire manifest performance even with the severe pixel class imbalance.
