A Historical Review of AI-Based Music Composition: From Local Pattern Prediction to Holistic Audio Generation

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
인공지능연구 논문지 7(3), 37–66

AI-based music composition has evolved from explicit rule encoding and local symbolic prediction to end-to-end generation of integrated musical audio. This historical review examines representative systems that either introduced a distinct generative paradigm or achieved practical deployment. It traces the progression from rule-based and stochastic models through recurrent networks and Transformers to waveform, latent-audio, diffusion, flow-matching, hybrid, and multimodal approaches. Early models reproduced local transitions among notes, chords, and rhythms but struggled with long-range form and thematic development. Recurrent and attention-based architectures expanded temporal context, yet largely remained within symbolic prediction. Audio-level models shifted the generative target from musical instructions to sound itself, enabling joint modeling of structure, performance, timbre, texture, and acoustic realization. The review argues that the key historical transition was not simply an increase in model capacity, but a change in representational scope: from decomposing music into human-defined units to learning high-dimensional relationships more holistically. Current systems still face unresolved problems in long-term planning, fine-grained control, editability, and human–AI collaboration. Nevertheless, their development reflects a broader movement from automating compositional procedures toward modeling increasingly integrated stages of musical ideation and sound formation.

Recent Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Logistics: An Evidence-Oriented Operational Review of Studies Published Since 2024

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
Preprints.org · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

Artificial intelligence (AI) research in logistics has rapidly shifted since 2024 toward operationally specific systems for forecasting, routing, warehouse optimization, supply-chain visibility, port scheduling, and smart-port maintenance. This paper presents an evidence-oriented operational review of studies published since 2024 on AI applications in logistics. Instead of classifying the literature by model type alone, the review organizes studies by logistics decision function and evaluates the evidence profile of each application: whether real operational data were used, whether the study relied on simulation or benchmark instances, whether the data scale was reported, and whether field validation was conducted. The analysis shows that recent AI logistics studies increasingly address concrete operational tasks, including demand forecasting, late-delivery prediction, route deviation prediction, dynamic vehicle routing, warehouse order picking, robotic fulfillment scheduling, vessel arrival prediction, berth allocation, quay crane scheduling, container dwell-time prediction, and predictive maintenance at smart ports. However, the evidence base remains uneven. Several studies use real operational or survey data, while many warehouse and routing studies rely on simulation, generated instances, or benchmark settings. Field validation remains rare. The paper argues that the next stage of logistics AI research should move beyond model accuracy and report operational evidence: data provenance, data scale, logistics KPIs, field validation, integration requirements, and human oversight.

MCP Design Strategy for Plant-Factory Operation AI Agents

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
ResearchGate · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

Plant factories are increasingly equipped with sensors, cameras, automatic controllers, digital twins, and artificial intelligence models. However, integrating these components into a practical operation AI agent requires a safe interface between AI reasoning and facility-level control. This paper proposes an MCP design strategy for plant-factory operation AI agents. The focus is not on implementing a new autonomous agent or replacing existing control systems, but on designing the Model Context Protocol layer through which an agent can read plant-factory context, call diagnostic and simulation tools, follow standard operating prompts, and update supervisory operation parameters. In the proposed strategy, MCP resources represent sensor data, crop images, nutrient states, recipes, actuator states, logs, and digital-twin states; MCP tools represent diagnosis, prediction, simulation, and bounded parameter-update functions; and MCP prompts represent user-selectable standard operating procedure templates. The key argument is that MCP should not be used as a deterministic real-time controller for plant factories. Instead, it should serve as a supervisory parameter interface that modifies setpoints, recipes, schedules, thresholds, and policy parameters used by existing 24-hour automatic control systems. This paper presents resource URI schemes, tool risk classification, parameter-update tool schemas, prompt-resource-tool binding, safety tables, and closed-loop operation workflows for MCP-mediated plant-factory operation.

Responding to Suspected LLM-Written Hallucinated Peer Reviews A Claim-Level Audit Protocol

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
ResearchGate · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

Authors are often advised to respond to peer-review comments politely and point by point. However, this advice is insufficient when a formal review appears to be LLM-written or hallucinated: fluent, plausible, professionally formatted, but contradicted by the manuscript. This paper proposes a claim-level audit protocol for responding to suspected LLM-written hallucinated peer reviews. The protocol decomposes reviewer comments into atomic claims, classifies each claim as supported, partially supported, contradicted, generic, scope-mismatched, or not assessable, and maps each claim to manuscript evidence before selecting a response strategy. The protocol is illustrated using an anonymized case from an actual submission to a Korea Citation Index (KCI) candidate journal, in which one review contained multiple false missing-content claims. The goal is not to encourage accusations of AI use, but to help authors replace speculation with auditable evidence for revision, rebuttal, and editorial escalation.

When Post-Publication Modification Is Not Enough - A Case Study of Multi-Layer Scholarly Overlap and Delayed Retraction

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
ResearchGate · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

Post-publication integrity concerns are often treated as problems of textual similarity, missing citation, or minor correctable error. However, some cases involve deeper forms of scholarly overlap that cannot be remedied through limited post-publication modification. This paper presents a case study of a post-publication complaint involving a 2022 preprint and a subsequent 2023 journal article that was later retracted. The official retraction notice states that an editorial investigation confirmed a high level of overlap between the subsequent article and the prior publication, including similarities in methodology, figures, layout, paper structure, and overall study topic, without appropriate acknowledgement and citation. The notice also states that concerns regarding the origins of the underlying data could not be satisfactorily resolved, leading the Editorial Board to lose confidence in the reliability and originality of the article. Using this case, the paper proposes a multi-layer overlap taxonomy and an editorial remedy framework. The central argument is that post-publication modification may be insufficient when the integrity concern extends beyond wording to methodology, visual materials, manuscript structure, attribution, and data provenance. In such cases, correction or citation addition cannot substitute for a full editorial investigation and, where appropriate, retraction.

Can LLMs Review Scientific Papers? A Case of Hallucination and Grounding Failure in Journal Peer Review

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
ResearchGate · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

Maybe not. Large language models (LLMs) can produce fluent and plausible peer-review reports, but fluency is not scholarly judgment. This paper presents a case study of three formal peer-review reports received during an actual submission to a Korea Citation Index (KCI) candidate journal. One review claimed that key methodological and experimental details were missing, although the manuscript explicitly contained information about validation data, hardware, software frameworks, model configuration, parameter count, algorithmic procedure, and limitations. After the author reported these grounding failures to the editor, the problematic review was not relied upon in the final decision, and the manuscript was subsequently accepted and published. The case is compared with two more grounded human reviews and an exploratory source-grounded LLM-generated review. The source-grounded LLM review was more aligned with the manuscript, but still produced minor factual errors and overstatements. The case suggests that grounding improves LLM-assisted review, but does not eliminate hallucination or accountability gaps.

A Systematic Review of AI-Based Music Composition: Limitations and Breakthroughs

Byunghyun Ban (2026)
ResearchGate · Preprint (not peer-reviewed)

AI-based music composition has evolved rapidly, with research paradigms shifting from modeling local patterns to capturing global musical coherence. This paper examines how compositional AI has progressed from modeling local patterns to forming global musical ideas. Early machine learning models, based on state transition probabilities, were able to capture only local continuity between notes and chords, and thus failed to produce meaningful phrasing or large-scale musical structure. With the introduction of recurrent architectures, models began to incorporate temporal context, and transformer-based approaches further enabled the generation of musically coherent passages over longer spans. Nevertheless, these models still exhibit limitations in maintaining overall structural completeness and long-term musical direction. More recent approaches move beyond sequential prediction toward the simultaneous generation of musical structure and sonic characteristics. This shift reflects a transition from the accumulation of local decisions to the formation of a more holistic musical representation.

Era of RAG-as-a-Service for Pretrained AI Applications

Byunghyun Ban (2026)

CongNaMul: A Dataset for Advanced Image Processing of Soybean Sprouts

Byunghyun Ban, Donghun Ryu, Su-won Hwang (2023)
ICTC 2023

We present 'CongNaMul', a comprehensive dataset designed for various tasks in soybean sprouts image analysis. The CongNaMul dataset is curated to facilitate tasks such as image classification, semantic segmentation, decomposition, and measurement of length and weight. The classification task provides four classes to determine the quality of soybean sprouts: normal, broken, spotted, and broken and spotted, for the development of AI-aided automatic quality inspection technology. For semantic segmentation, images with varying complexity, from single sprout images to images with multiple sprouts, along with human-labelled mask images, are included. The label has 4 different classes: background, head, body, tail. The dataset also provides images and masks for the image decomposition task, including two separate sprout images and their combined form. Lastly, 5 physical features of sprouts (head length, body length, body thickness, tail length, weight) are provided for image-based measurement tasks. This dataset is expected to be a valuable resource for a wide range of research and applications in the advanced analysis of images of soybean sprouts. Also, we hope that this dataset can assist researchers studying classification, semantic segmentation, decomposition, and physical feature measurement in other industrial fields, in evaluating their models. The dataset is available at the authors’ repository (https://bhban.kr/data).

Deep learning method to remove chemical, kinetic and electric artifacts on ISEs

Byunghyun Ban (2020)
ICTC

I suggest a deep learning based sensor signal processing method to remove chemical, kinetic and electrical artifacts from ion selective electrodes’ measured values. An ISE is used to investigate the concentration of a specific ion from aqueous solution, by measuring the Nernst potential along the glass membrane. However, application of ISE on a mixture of multiple ion has some problem. First problem is a chemical artifact which is called ion interference effect. Electrically charged particles interact with each other and flows through the glass membrane of different ISEs. Second problem is the kinetic artifact caused by the movement of the liquid. Water molecules collide with the glass membrane causing abnormal peak values of voltage. The last artifact is the interference of ISEs. When multiple ISEs are dipped into same solution, one electrode’s signal emission interference voltage measurement of other electrodes. Therefore, an ISE is recommended to be applied on single-ion solution, without any other sensors applied at the same time. Deep learning approach can remove both 3 artifacts at the same time. The proposed method used 5 layers of artificial neural networks to regress correct signal to remove complex artifacts with one-shot calculation. Its MAPE was less than 1.8% and R2 of regression was 0.997. A randomly chosen value of AI processed data has MAPE less than 5% (p-value 0.016).

The phenotype control kernel of a biomolecular regulatory network

Sang-Mok Choo, Byunghyun Ban, Jae Il Joo, Kwang-Hyun Cho* (2018)
BMC Systems Biology  (SCI)

Background: Controlling complex molecular regulatory networks is getting a growing attention as it can provide a systematic way of driving any cellular state to a desired cell phenotypic state. A number of recent studies suggested various control methods, but there is still deficiency in finding out practically useful control targets that ensure convergence of any initial network state to one of attractor states corresponding to a desired cell phenotype. Results: To find out practically useful control targets, we introduce a new concept of phenotype control kernel (PCK) for a Boolean network, defined as the collection of all minimal sets of control nodes having their fixed state values that can generate all possible control sets which eventually drive any initial state to one of attractor states corresponding to a particular cell phenotype of interest. We also present a detailed method with which we can identify PCK in a systematic way based on the layered network and converging tree of a given network. We identify all candidates for control nodes from the layered network and then hierarchically search for all possible minimal sets by using the converging tree. We show the usefulness of PCK by applying it to cell proliferation and apoptosis signaling networks and comparing the results with other control methods. PCK is the unique control method for Boolean network models that can be used to identify all possible minimal sets of control nodes. Interestingly, many of the minimal sets have only one or two control nodes. Conclusions: Based on the new concept of PCK, we can identify all possible minimal sets of control nodes that can drive any molecular network state to one of multiple attractor states representing a same desired cell phenotype.

Contemporary machine learning based segmentation techniques depend on statistical approach to minimize the objective functions which ensure statistically to distinguish each pixel into one of the desired classes. However, thus philosophy consistently fails when the numbers of pixels for those class meet severe imbalance and presents prediction errors on all around the given input. This thesis provides a robust solution for Bio-medical image segmentation with adversarial networks, which are composed of a segmentor based on fully convolutional neural networks, image windowing technique and a discriminator which interferes the segmentor’s learning, to acquire manifest performance even with the severe pixel class imbalance.
